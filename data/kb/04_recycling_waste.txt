TOPIC: Recycling Guidelines, Waste Reduction and Composting
SOURCE: Waste Management and Circular Economy Guide

== RECYCLING BASICS ==
Only about 32% of waste in the UK is recycled; the global average is even lower. Improving recycling starts with knowing what goes where.

COMMONLY RECYCLABLE (most UK/US kerbside collections):
- Paper and cardboard (flattened)
- Glass bottles and jars (rinsed)
- Metal tins and cans (rinsed)
- Plastic bottles, pots, tubs, trays (check local council – varies)
- Cartons (Tetra Pak): accepted at many recycling points

NOT RECYCLABLE KERBSIDE (common mistakes):
- Greasy pizza boxes (the grease contaminates paper recycling) – tear off clean lid, recycle it; bin the base
- Black plastic food trays (many sorting machines cannot detect black plastic)
- Crisp packets, sweet wrappers (soft plastics) – take to supermarket soft-plastic collection points
- Polystyrene (Styrofoam) – check for specialist recyclers
- Coffee cups (most are lined with plastic) – return to participating coffee shops
- Ceramics and pyrex – cannot be melted with glass bottles (different melting point)
- Nappies and tissues – landfill/residual waste

SOFT PLASTICS:
Many UK supermarkets (Tesco, Sainsbury's, Waitrose, Co-op) have in-store soft plastic collection points. Accepted items include carrier bags, bread bags, cereal bags, pasta bags, cling film, and bubble wrap.

ELECTRICAL WASTE (WEEE):
- Never put electronics in general waste or recycling – contains hazardous materials.
- Return to retailer (large retailers must take back old electronics when selling new equivalent).
- Drop off at Household Waste Recycling Centres (HWRCs/tips).
- Some councils offer WEEE doorstep collection.

BATTERIES:
- Do not put loose batteries in general waste (fire hazard in vehicles).
- Drop off at supermarket collection points, DIY stores, or recycling centres.
- Rechargeable batteries: use Batteryback scheme in the UK.

COMPOSTING:
Home composting diverts food and garden waste from landfill, where it would produce methane (28x more potent than CO2 over 100 years).

WHAT TO COMPOST:
- Fruit and vegetable peelings, cores, and scraps
- Tea bags (paper ones) and coffee grounds
- Eggshells
- Grass clippings, dead plants, leaves
- Cardboard (torn into small pieces), newspaper, paper bags
- Hair and nail clippings

WHAT NOT TO COMPOST:
- Meat, fish, dairy (attract pests)
- Cooked foods
- Diseased plants
- Pet waste (cat/dog)
- Glossy paper

BOKASHI SYSTEM: Ferments all food waste including meat and dairy using anaerobic bacteria. Output is then buried in soil to fully compost. Ideal for flats and small gardens.

WORMERIES (Vermicomposting):
Worms process kitchen scraps faster than traditional composting. Produces nutrient-rich worm castings (top-grade plant fertiliser) and liquid feed.

FOOD WASTE REDUCTION:
1. Plan meals weekly and write a shopping list before buying.
2. Understand date labels: "Use by" = safety; "Best before" = quality (food safe after this date).
3. Store food correctly (most fruit and veg last longer in the fridge).
4. First-in, first-out principle: move older items to front of fridge/cupboard.
5. Use apps: OLIO (share surplus with neighbours), Too Good To Go (buy surplus from restaurants/shops).
6. UK households waste £800/year on food on average – reducing waste saves money AND emissions.

WASTE HIERARCHY (in order of preference):
1. Refuse (don't buy/accept unnecessary items)
2. Reduce (use less)
3. Reuse (use again before disposing)
4. Repair (fix instead of replace)
5. Recycle (when above options exhausted)
6. Recover (energy from waste incineration)
7. Landfill (last resort)

GOVERNMENT SCHEMES:
- UK Simpler Recycling (from 2026): All English councils must collect the same materials – glass, metal, plastic, paper, card, food waste.
- UK Deposit Return Scheme (DRS): Planned for drinks containers (cans, glass, PET plastic).
- UK Right to Repair Regulations (2021): Manufacturers of appliances must supply spare parts for 7–10 years.
- EU Circular Economy Action Plan: Targets 55% municipal waste recycled by 2025.
