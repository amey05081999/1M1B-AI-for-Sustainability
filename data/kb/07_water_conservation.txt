TOPIC: Water Conservation and Reducing Water Footprint
SOURCE: Water Stewardship and Home Water Guide

== WHY WATER CONSERVATION MATTERS ==
Only 3% of Earth's water is fresh water; two-thirds of that is frozen in glaciers. Rising temperatures and population growth are putting unprecedented pressure on freshwater supplies. In the UK alone, the average person uses 142 litres per day. Water scarcity already affects 2 billion people globally.

INDOOR WATER SAVING TIPS:
1. Fix leaks promptly: A dripping tap at one drop per second wastes 10,000 litres/year.
2. Install a low-flow showerhead (reduces flow from 15 l/min to 8 l/min, saving ~2,000 litres/month for a family of four).
3. Take showers instead of baths (shower: ~35 litres; bath: ~80 litres).
4. Turn off the tap while brushing teeth – saves 6 litres per minute.
5. Use a dishwasher on a full load eco cycle (uses ~10 litres vs ~50 litres handwashing).
6. Run washing machine on full loads only; use the eco cycle.
7. Keep a jug of water in the fridge to avoid running the tap until it's cold.
8. Install a dual-flush toilet cistern (full flush 6 litres; half flush 3 litres vs old 13-litre cistern).
9. Place a hippo water saver or filled plastic bottle in older cisterns to reduce volume used per flush.

OUTDOOR WATER SAVING:
1. Install a water butt to collect rainwater for garden use (200-litre capacity typical).
2. Water plants in the early morning or evening to minimise evaporation.
3. Use mulch (bark, wood chips, compost) around plants to retain soil moisture.
4. Choose drought-tolerant plants and native species that require less watering.
5. Use a watering can rather than a hosepipe – hosepipes can use 1,000 litres per hour.
6. If using a hosepipe, attach a trigger nozzle to stop flow when not actively spraying.
7. Collect water from boiling vegetables and let cool – use to water plants (contains nutrients).
8. Grey-water systems recycle bath and sink water to flush toilets or water gardens.

VIRTUAL WATER (EMBEDDED WATER IN PRODUCTS):
- A beef burger: ~2,400 litres
- A cotton t-shirt: ~2,700 litres
- A cup of coffee: ~140 litres
- A glass of milk: ~200 litres
- A smartphone: ~12,000 litres
- 1 kg of rice: ~2,500 litres

Reducing meat consumption and buying fewer, more durable products is one of the most effective ways to reduce your water footprint.

WATER METERING:
1. Request a free water meter from your supplier (most UK water companies offer this).
2. Metered households use ~10–15% less water on average (people conserve more when they can see what they're using and paying for).
3. Monitor consumption with smart water meters to detect hidden leaks (e.g., constant slow consumption overnight indicates a leak).

GOVERNMENT WATER SCHEMES:
- UK Water Company Free Products: Most UK water companies (Thames Water, Yorkshire Water, Severn Trent, etc.) offer free water-saving gadgets – shower timers, tap aerators, hose nozzles, toilet cistern bags. Apply via your water company's website.
- UK Water Efficiency Labelling Scheme (WELS): Labels on showers, taps, toilets, and appliances show water efficiency rating.
- Australian WELS: Mandatory water efficiency labelling on plumbing products sold in Australia, 1–6 star rating.
- US WaterSense Label (EPA): Products and services meeting EPA water efficiency and performance criteria.
- EU Water Framework Directive: Sets quality standards and conservation targets for all EU member states.
