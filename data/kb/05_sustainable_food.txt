TOPIC: Sustainable Diet, Food Choices, and Plant-Based Eating
SOURCE: Food Systems and Climate Impact Guide

== FOOD AND CLIMATE ==
The global food system accounts for ~26% of total greenhouse gas emissions. Animal products (meat and dairy) contribute ~60% of food emissions despite providing only 18% of global calories.

CARBON FOOTPRINT OF COMMON FOODS (kg CO2e per kg of food):
- Beef: 60 kg CO2e/kg (highest)
- Lamb: 24 kg CO2e/kg
- Cheese: 21 kg CO2e/kg
- Pork: 7 kg CO2e/kg
- Chicken: 6 kg CO2e/kg
- Farmed salmon: 6 kg CO2e/kg
- Eggs: 4.5 kg CO2e/kg
- Rice: 4 kg CO2e/kg
- Tofu: 3 kg CO2e/kg
- Milk: 3.2 kg CO2e/kg
- Oat milk: 0.9 kg CO2e/kg
- Lentils: 0.9 kg CO2e/kg
- Vegetables (seasonal, local): 0.1–0.5 kg CO2e/kg

DIETARY SHIFTS:
1. Meat-free Mondays: Replacing one beef meal per week saves ~1,300 kg CO2e/year per person.
2. Moving to a fully vegan diet saves ~1.5 tonnes CO2e/year.
3. Moving to a vegetarian diet saves ~0.8 tonnes CO2e/year.
4. Reducing dairy consumption and switching to plant milks (oat, almond, soy) significantly lowers footprint.
5. Eating the most carbon-efficient animal proteins: white fish, shellfish, chicken, eggs.

SEASONAL AND LOCAL EATING:
1. Buy seasonal produce – out-of-season produce is often air-freighted (avocados in winter, berries in December). Air freight is 50x more carbon-intensive than sea freight.
2. Shop at farmers' markets or join a Community Supported Agriculture (CSA) scheme.
3. Grow your own herbs, salad leaves, tomatoes – even in a windowbox.
4. Choose British/local versions of produce when available.

ORGANIC FOOD:
- Organic farming avoids synthetic fertilisers (production emits N2O, a potent GHG) and pesticides.
- Organic farming builds soil carbon, supports biodiversity, and reduces water pollution.
- A diet partly organic + reduced meat can be both eco-friendly and affordable.

SUPERMARKET SHOPPING TIPS:
1. Shop with a list to avoid over-buying and food waste.
2. Loose fruit and veg typically has less packaging than pre-packed.
3. Choose Fairtrade certified products for coffee, tea, chocolate, and bananas.
4. Look for the Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) blue tick on seafood.
5. Choose Rainforest Alliance or UTZ certified products (coffee, cocoa, tea).
6. Pick whole foods over heavily processed ones (less packaging, often less carbon).

PLANT-BASED PROTEIN ALTERNATIVES:
- Lentils, chickpeas, beans: extremely low carbon, high protein, cheap.
- Tofu and tempeh: versatile, nutritious, widely available.
- Seitan (wheat gluten): high protein, very low carbon footprint.
- Quorn (mycoprotein): UK-made, low-carbon, available widely in supermarkets.
- Oat-based products: porridge oats as a cheap, nutritious, low-carbon staple.

KITCHEN PRACTICES:
1. Use a pressure cooker or Instant Pot to reduce cooking time and energy by up to 70%.
2. Microwave and air fryers are more energy-efficient than conventional ovens for small portions.
3. Match pot size to hob size; always put a lid on pots when boiling.
4. Batch cook and freeze meals to reduce energy use from frequent cooking.
5. Grow herbs on a windowsill (basil, mint, chives) – zero food miles, zero packaging.

GOVERNMENT FOOD SCHEMES:
- UK Henry Dimbleby National Food Strategy recommends a 30% reduction in meat consumption by 2030.
- EU Farm to Fork Strategy: Targets 50% reduction in pesticide use, 20% reduction in fertiliser use by 2030.
- UK Healthy Start vouchers support low-income families to buy fruit and vegetables.
- US Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP) can be used at farmers' markets in many states.
